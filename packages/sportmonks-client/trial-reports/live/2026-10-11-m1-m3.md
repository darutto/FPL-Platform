# FI-9 criterion (f) — M1, M2 and M3 on live data, checked against what happened (2026-10-11)

Aggregate only: counts and rates, no names, no ids. The people behind every number are in a local
file that is not committed; the short table Leo is asked to judge is
[2026-10-11-m1-m3-review-for-leo.md](2026-10-11-m1-m3-review-for-leo.md).

## What was run

- Store: the live-trial store built from one frozen capture (72 Sportmonks requests, taken 2026-10-11
  in the morning, local time): 88 clubs, 483 fixtures in 6 competitions, the Premier League base with
  380 fixtures, 520 identified players, 1678 appearances, 460 substitutions. Every store is
  `live_trial_local`; nothing was published.
- Modules: `evaluate_tactical_role` (M2), `evaluate_expected_minutes` (M1) and `evaluate_fixture_context`
  (M3), unchanged, with the `role-map-v3` of #424. Feature build: 936 s for the whole season's targets.
- **Hold-out: gameweek 6.** History strictly before each fixture's kickoff (so gameweeks 1 to 5 for the
  six gameweek-6 fixtures that had finished at capture time). Six fixtures, 126 starters with an
  identity (about one starter per match is lost to the identity quarantine). Re-run after the other
  four gameweek-6 fixtures and after gameweek 7.

| Out of position, partial (score 0.5) | 7 |

The nine role disagreements: four where the player actually played **attacking midfield** and the
history said something else (the role is new in v3 and few players have it in their last starts), two
predicted full-back who played centre-back (back-three systems), and three single cases. The seven
partial out-of-position flags: **four are FPL defenders whom the provider lists as Right/Left Midfield
(wing-backs)**: the decision of 2026-10-11 to treat those labels as wingers makes a wing-back look
half out of position. That is a consequence of the decision and not a finding about the players.

**The six Arsenal starters Leo confirmed against Sofascore** (two centre-backs, two central midfielders,
a right winger, a left winger): the position actually played matches the confirmed side and role in
**6 of 6**; the module's own prediction from history matches in **5 of 5 that it scored** (one
abstained).

### The baseline: "the same position as his last start" (added 2026-10-11, #429)

On the same 95 starters the module answered for:

| | Module | Baseline |
|---|---|---|
| Role = role actually played | 86 / 95 = 90.5 % | **90 / 95 = 94.7 %** |
| Flank (where a side is involved) | 25 / 34 = 73.5 % | **27 / 34 = 79.4 %** |
| Right where the other is wrong | 0 | 4 |

The baseline also answers for more starters (114 of 126, role 106 / 114 = 93.0 %; the module needs three
mapped starts). **M2 does not improve on "repeat the last match" as a predictor of the next role; its value is
in the distribution, the stability and the change detection, and that has not been measured yet.** Two
readings, both true: the baseline is built from the same Sportmonks fields (the last start's detailed
position and grid), so 94.7 % confirms the value of the *data*, which is what is being bought; and with six
fixtures early in the season, where the last start dominates, the sample favours the baseline. The runs of
2026-10-12 (ten fixtures) and 2026-10-18 (gameweek 7) report the cells where the module and the baseline
differ and who is right in them, and how many role changes since the last start the module saw coming.

**Proposal for FI-10 (calibration after the trial), not a change during it:** a recency-weighted role window
and a shorter one, tested against this baseline. M2 is not changed during the trial.

Large misses: **10** players predicted at 0.8 or more who did not start (5 of them are listed by FPL as
unavailable at capture time), and 1 predicted at 0.1 or less who did.

Two limits that matter for how far to trust this:

1. **Coverage.** The module abstains for 132 of 309, and **every one of them has zero starts in its window**
   (67 have no appearance at all, 65 appear only as substitutes). Mostly players who do not start, but
   **12 of the 126 starters (9.5 %) fell in that group** and 41 of the abstained players played some
   minutes: newcomers and returners, the cases a coach's surprise comes from.
2. **Sample.** One gameweek, six fixtures, 177 scored pairs: the 0.1-0.3 band (27 players, observed 44 %
   against 25 % predicted) is the visible miscalibration; with this n it is a flag, not a verdict.

## What the numbers do not say (risks)

- **R6, scheduling knowledge is assumed.** The provider keeps no history of what was known when. The
  store takes every fixture list as published on 2026-06-01 and every result as known three hours after
  kickoff. Effects: postponements and rescheduling are invisible, and M1/M2 windows and M3 congestion
  can include a result a few hours before it was really known.
- **M1 abstains where surprises come from** (above), and the hold-out assumes everyone available.
- **One gameweek.** Everything for M1 and M2 is six fixtures; it should be re-run on ten, then on
  gameweek 7.
- **Standings are recomputed** from results (not the provider's table); before the first result the
  league band is `unknown`, which is why M3 abstains twice.
- **Not evaluated:** canonical statistics (no type-id map), injuries and suspensions as canonical rows
  (availability enters M1 as an input), coaches, referees.
- **Build time.** 936 s for a full-season feature build; correct but slow, left alone.
